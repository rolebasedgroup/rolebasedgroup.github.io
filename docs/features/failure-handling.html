Failure Handling

RBG supports None and RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart. For the recreate policy, restartPolicyConfig.activateAfter decides when its triggers are armed.

Restart Policy Types​

PolicyDescription
NoneNo automatic restart action; rely on default pod restart behavior. Failed pods are replaced through normal reconciliation.
RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestartRecreate the whole role instance when a member fails, restarts unexpectedly, or is lost while terminating. activateAfter decides whether that starts before or after the instance is first Ready.

Triggers​

RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart recreates the instance on any of:

  • a desired member Pod that is Terminating,
  • a Pod in Failed phase,
  • a container restart outside the Pod's in-place-update exemption and recorded restart allowance.

Succeeded phase alone does not trigger recreation. A desired member observed with a deletionTimestamp can trigger it regardless of phase, including Succeeded. A member removed by scale-in (ordinal outside the desired component size) is not a desired member.

A Terminating member is the earliest signal after an eviction: with an unreachable node the Pod stays Terminating until the node comes back, the Node object is deleted, an out-of-service taint is applied, or it is force-deleted, and no same-name replacement exists before that. A member that disappears without ever being observed as Terminating (force delete, PodGC, controller downtime) is not covered by this trigger; missing/unobserved termination handling is deferred to #562.

The rollout guard (currentRevision not yet equal to updateRevision) blocks recreation, and a generation not yet observed by the controller also blocks it. With activateAfter: Ready, a spec change invalidates the previous generation's readiness latch. Pending or NotReady alone is not a trigger; a NotReady timeout is deferred to #380. Init-container and native-sidecar restarts do not count. A Pod with its InPlaceUpdateReady condition False is exempt from container-restart detection for that Pod, not just from restarts proven expected by baselines. Otherwise, recorded in-place-update baselines allow expected restarts. Failed phase and desired-member deletion are checked before this exemption and remain triggers.

Arming: activateAfter​

ValueMeaning
Ready (default)Triggers are armed once the instance has been Ready at its current generation. Creation and scale-up keep their protection.
CreatedTriggers are armed as soon as the instance exists, which also covers failures before the instance first becomes Ready.

The stability fact is persisted as status.lastReadyGeneration. Gating on the live RoleInstanceReady condition instead would be unsound: status is written after scaling, so a fault flips the condition to False before the restart signal becomes visible, and the policy would then never fire again. The Ready gate also retains the existing current-Ready and recent-restart bypasses, subject to the generation and revision guards.

A replacement that also fails before Ready is recovered again, with either activation mode. The Restarting guard is scoped to one recreation wave: it blocks only while the pre-restart Pods are still being deleted. Once they are all gone the wave completes, the guard is released and the next failure starts another whole-instance recreation, bounded by the exponential backoff (#563). With the default Ready activation this applies through the latched lastReadyGeneration and the recent-restart bypass; with Created it applies from instance creation. The exact release point holds for the in-memory wave marker; after a controller restart mid-wave, the persisted fallback uses creation timestamps with second granularity and may release the guard early for a same-second pre-restart Pod, allowing at most one extra backoff-bounded recreation.

activateAfter is supported by native RoleInstanceSet workloads. LeaderWorkerSet-backed roles keep the policy they had; their behavior is already ungated (LWS recreates the group on ContainerRestarted or PodDeleted with no start or Ready gate) and the vendored LWS v0.7 adapter exposes no field to express the distinction. StatefulSet and Deployment roles have no instance-level recreate path.

Deleting a single member now rebuilds the whole RoleInstance. That is the intended semantics for this policy — the members of a multi-rank workload agree on a one-shot membership at startup, so replacing one rank in place does not recover it — and it matches LeaderWorkerSet. Use the restart-trigger-policy: Ignore annotation on auxiliary components whose failures should not affect the main workload.

Both served RBG APIs accept the existing restart policy, but only v1alpha2 expresses activateAfter, in the pattern's restartPolicyConfig. v1alpha1 has only spec.roles[].restartPolicy. A GET through v1alpha1 omits activateAfter from the response; it neither drops the persisted configuration nor changes running behavior. A read-modify-write through v1alpha1 drops the setting and resolves to the default Ready, as it already does for the backoff delays. Use v1alpha2 to preserve these settings.

Configuration​

Set the restartPolicyConfig field on the pattern (leaderWorkerPattern or customComponentsPattern). standalonePattern has no restart policy field — it is always None (single pod, recreating the instance is equivalent to normal pod replacement).

restartPolicyConfig is an object with the following fields:

FieldTypeDefaultDescription
typestringRecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestartRestart policy type: None or RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart.
activateAfterstringReadyWhen the recreate triggers are armed: Ready or Created. Omit it to keep the default.
baseDelaySecondsint30Base delay (seconds) for exponential backoff between restart attempts. Set to 0 with maxDelaySeconds: 0 to disable backoff entirely.
maxDelaySecondsint600Cap (seconds) for the exponential backoff delay.

Deprecated restartPolicy field​

The pattern also accepts a deprecated restartPolicy string field, which is the shape v0.7.0 used before the backoff configuration was introduced. It is still honoured so that workloads written for v0.7.0 keep working:

  1. If restartPolicyConfig.type is set, it is used.
  2. Otherwise, if restartPolicy is set, its value is used.
  3. Otherwise the pattern default (RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart) applies.

The two fields are resolved at runtime — the controller and the conversion webhook always read them through the precedence above, and stored objects are not modified. New workloads should only set restartPolicyConfig.

Upgrading​

  • From v0.7.0: no action required. The restartPolicy wire format is unchanged.
  • From v0.8.0-alpha.3 / v0.8.0-alpha.4: the released images for these two pre-releases briefly defined restartPolicy as an object instead of a string. That shape is no longer decodable by v0.8.0+ controllers — leaving even one object in the old shape prevents the new controller from listing that resource type at all, not just the offending object. These two versions are unsupported and should not be used. If you deployed them, delete all affected objects (RoleBasedGroup, RoleBasedGroupSet, RoleInstance, RoleInstanceSet) and recreate them with v0.8.0+.
  • Serialized-template compatibility: activateAfter is omitted unless explicitly set; its Ready default is resolved at runtime, not by a CRD default. The status-only readiness latch is not hashed. This avoids an upgrade-only template change/default materialization and rollout, not runtime behavior changes: the default recreate policy intentionally gains the readiness latch and Terminating-member trigger.
  • Setting or changing activateAfter: changes the serialized RoleInstance template and can roll the role. With RecreatePod, this can delete and recreate every RoleInstance and rebuild all its Pods, even when explicitly setting Ready where the field was previously omitted. With InPlaceIfPossible, a policy-only change updates RoleInstance specs in place without restarting Pods.
  • Terminating member at upgrade: a Ready instance that already has a Terminating desired member when the new controller starts (for example during a node drain) can be rebuilt on the first reconcile. Existing instances stuck NotReady without a readiness latch are not rescued automatically: manually delete the RoleInstance, not just one Pod, to recover. status.lastReadyGeneration only starts latching after Ready is observed.
  • Older writers and controller rollback: a v1alpha1 read-modify-write drops activateAfter (as it drops the backoff delays today). A rolled-back controller or another older writer may also drop it when writing the object/template. Merely rolling back the binary does not itself rewrite persisted objects, but losing the serialized field on a subsequent write changes the template and can roll the role again, rebuilding all Pods under RecreatePod.
apiVersion: workloads.x-k8s.io/v1alpha2
kind: RoleBasedGroup
metadata:
  name: restart-policy-demo
spec:
  roles:
    # Worker role with leaderWorkerPattern - recreate instance on pod failure (default)
    - name: worker
      replicas: 3
      leaderWorkerPattern:
        size: 2
        restartPolicyConfig:
          type: RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart
        template:
          spec:
            containers:
              - name: worker
                image: nginx:latest

    # Worker role with explicit None - no restart action
    - name: auxiliary
      replicas: 1
      leaderWorkerPattern:
        size: 2
        restartPolicyConfig:
          type: None
        template:
          spec:
            containers:
              - name: auxiliary
                image: nginx:latest

    # StandalonePattern - no restart policy field (always None)
    - name: monitor
      replicas: 1
      standalonePattern:
        template:
          spec:
            containers:
              - name: monitor
                image: nginx:latest

Exponential Backoff​

When the resolved restart policy type is RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart, subsequent crash-triggered recreations within a stability window are delayed by exponential backoff:

  • First crash: immediate recreation (no delay).
  • Second crash (within the stability window): delayed by baseDelaySeconds.
  • Each subsequent crash: delay doubles, capped at maxDelaySeconds.
  • Formula: delay = min(baseDelaySeconds * 2^(restartCount-1), maxDelaySeconds).

The stability window is max(maxDelaySeconds * 2, 10 minutes). If the instance remains stable (no crashes) for longer than this window, restartCount resets to 0 and the next crash starts with immediate recreation again.

Backoff does not override activation. The Restarting guard only absorbs the triggers produced while the pre-restart Pods of the in-flight recreation are deleted; once that wave completes, backoff bounds any further recreations, including pre-Ready failures with Created (#563).

Default Behavior Change​

Note: Before the backoff feature, pod crashes always triggered immediate recreation. With default settings (baseDelaySeconds: 30, maxDelaySeconds: 600), the second and subsequent crashes within the stability window now receive 30s→600s exponential delays. All existing workloads using the default RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart policy automatically inherit this behavior.

Disabling Backoff​

To disable backoff delays for eligible recreations, set both baseDelaySeconds and maxDelaySeconds to 0. Activation arming still applies, but nothing delays recreations: a rebuilt group that keeps failing (before or after Ready) is recreated repeatedly, paced only by each wave's Pod deletion and creation cycle, and RestartCount grows unboundedly. Keep the defaults enabled if the workload can enter a crash-looping state:

restartPolicyConfig:
  type: RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart
  baseDelaySeconds: 0
  maxDelaySeconds: 0

Custom Backoff Configuration​

restartPolicyConfig:
  type: RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart
  baseDelaySeconds: 10
  maxDelaySeconds: 120

With the above configuration: first crash is immediate, second crash delayed 10s, third 20s, fourth 40s, fifth 80s, sixth and beyond capped at 120s.

Note: maxDelaySeconds must be greater than or equal to baseDelaySeconds (enforced by CEL validation at admission). Because maxDelaySeconds defaults to 600, setting baseDelaySeconds above 600 without explicitly setting maxDelaySeconds will be rejected. If you need a base delay above 600s, set maxDelaySeconds to the same or higher value.

Bounded Group Recovery​

Upgrade the CRDs, controller, and admission webhook together before configuring a budget. CRD CEL on RoleSpec and the existing v1alpha2 admission webhooks enforce the native RoleInstanceSet backend constraint for newly configured budgets on both leaderWorkerPattern and customComponentsPattern, in RoleBasedGroup and RoleBasedGroupSet. CRD CEL also validates the explicit policy type and non-negative limit; backend validation does not rely on the webhook alone.

The backend CEL rule is new in this release and Kubernetes 1.28 has no CRD validation ratcheting, so it also applies to updates of already stored objects. A role that an earlier controller with webhooks disabled admitted with maxGroupRestarts on a non-RoleInstanceSet role-workload-type rejects every later spec update until the budget is removed. Such a budget was never enforced. Before upgrading the CRDs, find these roles and remove maxGroupRestarts:

kubectl get rbg -A -o json | jq -r '.items[] | . as $g | .spec.roles[]
  | select(((.leaderWorkerPattern.restartPolicyConfig.maxGroupRestarts // .customComponentsPattern.restartPolicyConfig.maxGroupRestarts) != null)
      and ((.annotations["rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/role-workload-type"] // "") | (. != "" and . != "workloads.x-k8s.io/v1alpha2/RoleInstanceSet")))
  | "\($g.metadata.namespace)/\($g.metadata.name) role=\(.name)"'

Run the same query for rbgs (RoleBasedGroupSet) with .spec.groupTemplate.spec.roles[].

maxGroupRestarts is supported only by v1alpha2, like activateAfter and the backoff delays. Configure the budget through v1alpha2. A GET through v1alpha1 omits these fields but does not change the persisted object. A v1alpha1 read-modify-write of the spec drops them: an omitted budget means unlimited recovery, not the previous limit. Do not use v1alpha1 spec writes to manage a budgeted workload. This compatibility boundary is covered by conversion tests and the real API-server conversion path.

For native RoleInstanceSet workloads, set restartPolicyConfig.maxGroupRestarts on leaderWorkerPattern or customComponentsPattern (or on a directly managed RoleInstance's spec.restartPolicyConfig):

restartPolicyConfig:
  type: RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart
  activateAfter: Created
  maxGroupRestarts: 2

The optional non-negative limit applies to the native RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart policy with either activation setting. Specify type explicitly when setting a budget. None and backing LeaderWorkerSet, Deployment, and StatefulSet workloads do not support a budget. An unset limit preserves unbounded automatic recovery. 0 permits healthy initial creation, but stops recovery on the first eligible fault; 2 permits exactly two accepted whole-group rebuilds, then stops on the next eligible fault. Ignored components cannot trigger exhaustion, but are terminated with the complete group if another member triggers it.

Durable accounting and exhaustion​

The budget is per RoleInstance UID and component topology/Pod-template revision. The RI status carries groupRestartCount, groupRestartRevision, and restartBudgetExhausted. The budget revision combines the ControllerRevision name selected by the existing RI revision reconciler with a component-name-sorted size digest. It does not hash this binary's serialization of Pod templates again: semantic revision matching keeps the selected name stable across serialization-only upgrades, while real template changes select a new name and size changes alter the size digest. The selected revision is passed directly into budget processing, not inferred from potentially lagging status.updateRevision.

Upgrading from v0.9.0-alpha.5 or earlier builds. Those builds stored groupRestartRevision as a bare SHA-256 digest of the component JSON. On its first budget reconcile the new controller re-stamps such a value in place with the current identity. The re-stamp keeps groupRestartCount, restartBudgetExhausted, conditions, retained Pods and any recovery request; it is not treated as a topology change, so the upgrade neither grants a fresh budget nor releases an exhausted group without recover=true. A real topology change after the upgrade resets the budget as usual. A topology change that the old controller had not yet reconciled when it was replaced is attributed to the re-stamped revision; it does not release exhaustion, so recover such a group explicitly.

Accepted rebuilds consume one durable attempt only when the optimistic status compare-and-swap (CAS) commits, before any Pod deletion. Conflicted or otherwise uncommitted attempts, retries and suppressed triggers do not consume budget. A lost API response does not undo a committed increment: retries use the persisted count. The durable count is separate from the quiet-period-resetting restartCount used for backoff. Ready transitions, quiet periods, policy edits, and limit edits/removal do not reset it within the same revision. When no budget remains, exhaustion takes precedence over restart backoff.

API reads during budget reconciliation​

A normal Scale-to-Update pass shares one fresh budget observation, scoped to that reconcile and the RI UID, generation and selected revision. Direct Update calls still run their own budget checks. Healthy at-limit groups use the already listed active and inactive Pods only to decide whether a fault needs an uncached member-list recheck; accounting and exhaustion still require live confirmation. Already-retained or deleting Pods do not incur a per-Pod finalizer-check GET on steady exhausted polls. The final status-write read, restart-acceptance checks, and the authoritative empty-list recovery barrier are not removed. There is no cross-reconcile budget cache or change to the 30-second safety backstop. Recovery drain and finalizer release likewise skip per-Pod reads for live-listed Pods that are already Terminating or never carried the budget finalizer.

The informer snapshot can lag a newly Failed Pod. A healthy at-limit RI then marks exhaustion on the reconcile triggered by that Pod's watch event rather than the current one. This only delays exhaustion: an accepted rebuild is always revalidated against live state, so the lag cannot overspend the budget.

Configuration updates use the existing rollout path​

Changes to maxGroupRestarts, baseDelaySeconds, and maxDelaySeconds remain part of the RoleInstanceSet template and follow its existing revision/update strategy, just like other template changes. There is no separate synchronization to existing RoleInstances and no bypass of pause, partition, availability, or lifecycle controls. Applying a revision uses the recovery configuration stored in that revision.

A rollout may replace an RI, giving the replacement its own budget. When the RI is updated in place, changes to policy, budget, or backoff alone do not reset its current topology budget or clear exhaustion.

Size changes start a fresh budget, as in LWS​

Component sizes and their derived Pod-template labels participate in the budget revision. Changing group size creates a new topology/revision and follows the existing RoleInstanceSet rollout path, with a fresh budget for each new group. This also permits a new size revision to replace a budget-exhausted old group; it does not reset the old revision's accounting in place. No special resize identity-preservation path, member stamp or temporary-surge protocol is used. Existing pause, partition, availability, lifecycle and update-strategy controls continue to govern the rollout.

Changing the number of groups (replicas) is different: surviving instances keep their UID, revision and consumed budget, while newly created groups start at zero. A size/configuration rollout itself is not charged as a failure-recovery attempt.

On exhaustion, the controller first acquires the internal rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/group-restart-budget-cleanup finalizer on the RI, before persisting exhaustion or retaining/deleting Pods. A healthy budgeted RI does not acquire this cleanup finalizer. The RI reports Degraded=True, reason GroupRestartBudgetExceeded, and RoleInstanceReady=False. The controller stops replacement creation and in-place updates, adds the same finalizer to all owned Pods that can still receive it, then requests deletion of the complete group. Pods already deleting cannot acquire a new finalizer. Suppression is persisted in RI status and survives controller restarts, even if all retained Pods disappear. Already-exhausted reconciliation ensures the RI cleanup owner is present before retrying Pod retention/deletion, without clearing post-exhaustion recovery intent. Changing or removing the limit or tuning backoff on the same RI does not unfreeze it. Configuration changes through a RoleInstanceSet follow its normal rollout rules and may replace the RI, as noted above.

Finalizers retain Pod API objects and status, not running containers. Kubelet's Kubernetes 1.27+ normal deletion flow transitions Pods to a terminal phase and releases scheduled resources (including GPUs); the controller never synthesizes Pod phases. Native kubelet/CRI logs are best effort and are not guaranteed to remain available. Export diagnostics to an external logging system.

Explicit recovery and lifecycle cleanup​

After investigating and fixing the failure, annotate the RoleInstance, not an RBG, RoleInstanceSet, or Pod:

kubectl annotate roleinstance <instance-name> -n <namespace> \
  rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/recover=true --overwrite

In spec.roles[].annotations the annotation does not request recovery, and the controller does not copy it from there to the role's workload. It is still part of the role like any other role annotation, so adding or removing it there creates a new RoleBasedGroup revision.

Only the exact value true requests recovery. On a non-exhausted budget-managed RI, it is removed on the next reconcile; stale intent is also defensively removed before recording exhaustion, so recovery must be requested afterward. Recovery first retries any unfinished termination, then releases only the group's Pod budget finalizers. Suppression, counters and the recovery annotation remain in place until an authoritative API read finds no old owned Pod objects. A successful Delete request or an elapsed grace period alone is not completion. Other finalizers can hold recovery indefinitely and are never removed by RBG.

While explicit recovery or an exhausted topology-revision change waits at this barrier, Degraded=True keeps reason GroupRestartBudgetExceeded and its message reports how many old owned Pod objects remain, using the authoritative remaining Pod list after deletion/finalizer-release attempts. The waiting message changes only when that count changes, not as time passes. Generic status recomputation preserves it across reads and writes. RoleInstanceReady remains False with reason GroupRestartBudgetExceeded; waiting does not redefine Ready or add an API field.

Only after that barrier does the controller commit the status reset, clearing suppression, the durable count and backoff tracking, then remove the RI budget finalizer and consume the annotation to allow a fresh group. Failures or crashes are retried through the leftover RI finalizer and/or recovery annotation. If the status reset already committed, cleanup resumes without resetting the budget again. This prevents the old group's Terminating members from immediately charging or exhausting the new budget. Recovery is independent of Ready; an exhausted group need not and cannot be required to become Ready before recovery is allowed.

A new topology/Pod-template revision (including a size change) has the same old-group exit barrier and status-reset-before-finalizer-release ordering when updating an exhausted RI directly. Leftover cleanup intent remains retryable. RoleInstanceSet rollout replacement instead follows teardown cleanup below. Limit or policy edits alone do not change the budget revision.

For a non-exhausted RI, a topology revision change immediately resets only groupRestartRevision, groupRestartCount (to zero), restartBudgetExhausted (to false), and Degraded. It preserves Restarting, restartCount, lastRestartTime, and any in-memory recreation wave. The budget reset does not wait for that wave to complete: the existing #573 guard still absorbs the old wave's own Terminating triggers until its original Pod UIDs disappear.

Deleting an RI, selecting it for rollout replacement, or scaling it down is teardown, not recovery. An RI cleanup finalizer keeps cleanup retryable until all of its owned Pods have had budget finalizers removed. Teardown never charges another restart, clears the counter as a recovery side effect, or creates a new group. Other RI replicas remain independent and continue normal reconciliation.

Healthy RIs that retain a cleanup owner from an earlier implementation release owned Pod budget finalizers before removing the RI budget finalizer. Cleanup preserves all external RI and Pod finalizers.

Pod finalizer acquisition is limited to Pods still controlled by the original RI and matching its current member selector. If an operator changes Pod labels or removes its owner reference, RBG no longer manages that Pod; external cleanup is the operator's responsibility. The budget finalizer never grants authority over a foreign or same-name replacement Pod.

Budgeted accepted rebuilds emit ReCreateInstance with the policy, triggering Pod and live-validated reason, only after the accounting status transaction succeeds. Deferred, rejected and duplicate budget attempts do not emit another accepted-rebuild event. Unbudgeted event behavior is unchanged.

After a successful exhausted status CAS, the controller emits Warning GroupRestartBudgetExceeded with the live triggering Pod, reason, count and limit. After a successful exhausted-to-false status reset, it emits Normal GroupRestartBudgetRecovered for explicit recovery or a new topology revision. Retry polls do not repeat these transition events. Events are best effort, not exactly-once across crashes or lost API responses.

Controller downgrade prerequisites​

Do not roll back to a controller that lacks restart-budget support while any RoleInstance or Pod still carries rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/group-restart-budget-cleanup. The older controller cannot release this finalizer, so deletion and scale-down can remain stuck. Only RIs and Pods still carrying it require pre-downgrade cleanup/teardown; healthy never-exhausted and fully recovered RIs do not require forced teardown. Let the current controller release any legacy healthy cleanup owners as described above. Unsetting maxGroupRestarts is not a substitute for verifying cleanup.

For workloads with outstanding cleanup finalizers, before downgrading:

  1. Keep the budget-aware controller and current CRDs running.
  2. Scale the affected parent workloads to zero or remove them so they cannot recreate budgeted RIs. Normally delete any directly managed RIs carrying the budget finalizer, and let the current controller finish teardown cleanup.
  3. Verify that the budget finalizer is absent from both RIs and Pods in every affected namespace. Pods manually relabeled or detached from their owner require operator cleanup as described above.
  4. Remove budget settings from the source configuration before restoring replicas under the older controller, then downgrade only after cleanup has completed.

This procedure tears down workloads that still require teardown; it is not a workload-preserving downgrade for them. Do not blindly remove all finalizers; unrelated finalizers remain under their respective owners' control.

Rolling back to v0.9.0-alpha.5 after this controller has re-stamped budget revisions repeats the format mismatch in the other direction: alpha.5 compares its own bare digest with the re-stamped value and treats every budgeted RI as a topology change. Spent budgets are reset, and exhausted groups are released without recover=true: their retained Pods are deleted and the group is recreated. Collect anything you need from retained Pods before such a rollback. Upgrading again re-stamps the alpha.5 value in place without a further reset.

Remaining recovery limitations​

The budget does not add missing-member detection or retain a transient deletion signal through backoff; those are tracked in #562 and #566. Budgeted restarts reuse the wave guard and deletion retry helpers from #573: the guard ends when the original Pod UIDs disappear, without waiting for Ready. No additional Pod ledger is introduced. A NotReady timeout remains out of scope (#380).

Excluding Components from Restart Policy Trigger​

You can prevent specific pods from triggering the role's restart policy by setting an annotation on the pod template. This is useful for auxiliary components (e.g., monitoring, logging sidecars) whose failures should not affect the main workload. The annotation works with any deployment pattern (standalonePattern, leaderWorkerPattern, or customComponentsPattern).

Set the annotation rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/restart-trigger-policy: "Ignore" in the component's template.metadata.annotations:

apiVersion: workloads.x-k8s.io/v1alpha2
kind: RoleBasedGroup
metadata:
  name: inference-demo
spec:
  roles:
    - name: engine
      replicas: 2
      customComponentsPattern:
        restartPolicyConfig:
          type: RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart
        components:
          # Main inference component - will trigger restart policy on pod failure
          - name: inference
            size: 1
            template:
              spec:
                containers:
                  - name: inference
                    image: inference:latest

          # Monitoring sidecar - will NOT trigger restart policy on pod failure
          - name: monitor
            size: 1
            template:
              metadata:
                annotations:
                  # This component's pod restart/delete will NOT trigger role/RBG recreation
                  rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/restart-trigger-policy: "Ignore"
              spec:
                containers:
                  - name: monitor
                    image: monitoring:latest

When this annotation has the exact value "Ignore" on a component's pod template:

  • Failure, deletion and container restarts of that component's Pods do not initiate instance recreation.
  • Other components can still trigger the policy, and the resulting whole-instance recreation also deletes and rebuilds the ignored component's Pods.
  • Omitted values, "Inherit", and any value other than the exact "Ignore" do not suppress initiation.

Use Cases​

  • RecreateRoleInstanceOnPodRestart: tightly coupled workloads needing whole-instance recovery from unexpected ordinary-container restarts, Failed Pods, or a desired member observed Terminating; armed after Ready by default.
  • activateAfter: Created: instances that also need a recovery attempt before first Ready, such as a role whose startup can fail and then hang in a rendezvous. Repeated pre-Ready failures trigger repeated whole-instance recreations, bounded by the exponential backoff (#563).
  • None: Monitoring/logging sidecars that don't affect the main workload. Failed pods are replaced individually.
  • restart-trigger-policy: "Ignore" (annotation): Auxiliary components within a multi-component instance whose pod failures should not trigger the role's restart policy for the entire instance.
