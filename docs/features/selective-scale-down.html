Stateful Selective Scale-Down

Use the role annotation rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/excluded-ordinals to prefer specific instances for scale-down and avoid those ordinals during later scale-up. This Alpha capability supports native Stateful RoleInstanceSet roles on RBGs that are not managed by an RBGSet. Stateless roles, other workload backends, RBGSet templates, and RBGSet-managed RBGs do not support the annotation.

No policy or controller feature flag is required. Without the annotation and without previously committed sparse membership, scaling keeps its existing order.

Configure and scale​

Start with the example, which creates three members [0,1,2]:

kubectl apply -f examples/basic/rbg/scaling/selective-scale-down.yaml

Set exclusions and reduce replicas in the same update:

kubectl patch rbg selective-scaling --type=json -p='[
  {"op":"add","path":"/spec/roles/0/annotations","value":{"rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/excluded-ordinals":"1"}},
  {"op":"replace","path":"/spec/roles/0/replicas","value":2}
]'

The committed members become [0,2]. Setting exclusions before a separate replica edit is also supported. Editing exclusions alone never replaces an instance. Preserve other role annotations when adapting this example.

Read the child RIS once to observe both parts of the committed target:

kubectl get ris selective-scaling-worker -o json |
  jq '{replicas: .spec.replicas, skipped: .metadata.annotations["rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/committed-skip-ordinals"]}'

The scale-down is committed when this object shows replicas: 2 and skipped: "1". It does not mean the old instance has finished retiring. After commitment, scale back to three, retaining the exclusion:

kubectl patch rbg selective-scaling --type=json -p='[
  {"op":"replace","path":"/spec/roles/0/replicas","value":3}
]'

The new target is [0,2,3]. Instances 0 and 2 retain their identities. Rapid scale-down and scale-up edits before commitment can be coalesced by the controller and do not guarantee removal of instance 1.

Replacement through scaling temporarily reduces desired capacity by the number removed; serving capacity may remain reduced until replacements are ready. Wait for old instances to retire as well if replacement Pods need their GPU, memory, or other resources. Traffic draining, data migration and PVC cleanup remain separate operations. For leader/worker roles, one ordinal selects the entire leader/worker group, preserving the configured group size.

Selection rules​

Values accept individual nonnegative 32-bit ordinals and inclusive ranges, such as "1,3,8-10". Duplicates and overlaps are normalized; input order is not priority.

  • Scale-down removes only the required number: excluded members first, highest ordinal first within each priority, then the remaining highest ordinals.
  • Scale-up retains committed members and adds the lowest unused, nonexcluded ordinals. Initial creation uses this rule too.
  • At fixed replicas, editing or clearing the annotation leaves membership alone.
  • Missing or unhealthy instances are recovered at their committed ordinals.
  • Admission rejects invalid values and unsupported locations with a field and cause. If invalid input bypasses admission, reconciliation reports a Warning event and a failed Ready condition. Updates to the offending role stop without a default-order fallback; other roles and cleanup of removed workloads continue. Discovery retains that role's committed target, or publishes no members if the invalid role has never created a child.

For example, changing replicas from five to three and exclusions to "1,3" together preserves [0,2,4]. Exclusions can contain more or fewer members than the scale-down amount, or ordinals that have no current instance.

Rollout and autoscaling​

The existing rollout strategies and autoscaler replica ownership remain in use. Partition counts positions in the sorted committed member list: for [0,2,4] and partition: 2, 0 and 2 keep the old revision and 4 is eligible for upgrade. Changing only exclusions does not move this scope, change template revisions, or trigger a rollout. Template rollback preserves the current exclusion input and committed membership.

New rollout surge slots also skip the current exclusions, including ordinals above the last committed member. For members [0,2,4], E="1,3,5", and one surge slot, the new surge uses 6. The conditional writer mirrors E onto child RIS metadata for this allocation; this input copy is separate from committed membership and stays out of revisions and RI/Pod templates. Editing or removing E preserves already-created surge identities until normal rollout completion, subject to the configured surge count budget.

Discovery intentionally publishes the committed target immediately: selected members leave the discovery ConfigMap before their instances finish retiring. This is an early-drain signal, not a guarantee that consumers have stopped traffic or that the old instances have exited.

Disablement and downgrade​

Remove the role annotation to disable future exclusion-based selection. This keeps already committed members. For [0,2,3], clearing exclusions keeps [0,2,3]; a later scale-up to four can fill ordinal 1.

The controller stores the actual gaps under a distinct child RIS annotation, rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/committed-skip-ordinals, together with spec.replicas. This record is not a copy of the role's exclusion input.

Child RIS objects are controller-managed and must not be edited directly. Editing or deleting the internal annotation changes desired membership immediately and is not automatically recovered. No identity-based admission protection is added for it.

An old controller must not run against sparse RIS objects. Before downgrade, remove the role input, scale out over the gaps until every child RIS has an empty internal record, and wait for reconciliation to finish. All active controller replicas must understand the feature before exclusions are used.

Recover a corrupt committed record​

An unparseable committed-skip-ordinals record stops membership reconciliation and reports InvalidCommittedOrdinals. Removing E from the role does not repair the record. There is no automatic dense fallback: dropping the record can change desired identities and retire higher members.

This is an exceptional operator repair. Stop concurrent replica and template edits for the role, save the RBG/RIS/RoleInstance state, and establish the intended membership from a trusted previous committed target. Do not infer it solely from live instances: some may be missing, terminating, or surge instances. Check associated Pod/PVC ownership before changing membership.

The following example assumes role 0 is worker, the verified target has three members [0,2,4], and the corrupted annotation still exists. Use the appropriate namespace on every command. First remove E from the role if it is present:

kubectl get rbg selective-scaling -o yaml > rbg-before-repair.yaml
kubectl patch rbg selective-scaling --type=json -p='[
  {"op":"test","path":"/spec/roles/0/name","value":"worker"},
  {"op":"remove","path":"/spec/roles/0/annotations/rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io~1excluded-ordinals"}
]'
kubectl get ris selective-scaling-worker -o json > ris-before-repair.json
kubectl get ri -l rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io/group-name=selective-scaling -o yaml > instances-before-repair.yaml

Restore the verified record using UID, resourceVersion, and replica preconditions:

jq --arg record '1,3' --argjson replicas 3 '[
  {op:"test",path:"/metadata/uid",value:.metadata.uid},
  {op:"test",path:"/metadata/resourceVersion",value:.metadata.resourceVersion},
  {op:"test",path:"/spec/replicas",value:$replicas},
  (if $record == "" then
    {op:"remove",path:"/metadata/annotations/rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io~1committed-skip-ordinals"}
   else
    {op:"replace",path:"/metadata/annotations/rbg.workloads.x-k8s.io~1committed-skip-ordinals",value:$record}
   end)
]' ris-before-repair.json > membership-repair.json
kubectl patch ris selective-scaling-worker --type=json --patch-file=membership-repair.json

Use --arg record '' only when the verified intended target is dense [0,1,...,replicas-1] and retirement of any higher instances is understood and approved. This deletes the corrupt annotation with E absent, allowing ordinary reconciliation to resume. Never use deletion as a generic sparse-record repair.

If a precondition fails, reread the objects and reassess the target; do not remove the guards or replay a stale patch. Controllers resume immediately after the repair. Verify committed membership, survivor identities and discovery addresses, and confirm that the InvalidCommittedOrdinals condition clears. Unrelated FailedScale conditions are retained.
